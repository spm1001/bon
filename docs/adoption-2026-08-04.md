# How much do we actually use bon? — measured 2026-08-04

## Why this exists

A session read the bon board, noticed that nine of its ten outcomes are about bon's own machinery, and told Sameer the "maintenance ratio" tripwire from `.bon/understanding.md` might be firing — that we might be spending more sessions building bon than using it.

Sameer pushed back: *"If you were to do an analysis of all our conversations you would see Bon being used in most of them. Some of our most consistently used."*

He was right, and the error was one the session had already named as a risk in the same breath — it counted the numerator (plumbing sessions, all of which live in one repo) and guessed at the denominator (use, which is spread across the whole estate and invisible from inside bon's own repo). This is the measurement that settles it, plus the qualitative detail on *how* bon gets used, which turned out to be the more interesting half.

## The headline

> **Revised twice after first publication.** v1 read only tube's session store and only the bon CLI. Sameer then asked two questions — *"may have been different on Hezza?"* and *"would it be easier to look for Skill invocation?"* — and both changed the method. The numbers below are the two-host, two-instrument version. The single-host figures are kept in "Which hosts" so the size of that error stays on the record.

Pass over **8,848 CC session transcripts** across **tube and kube**, Dec 2025 – 4 Aug 2026. 3,537 are substantive (≥3 assistant turns); the rest are trivial or aborted openings.

Two independent instruments, unioned:

- **A — CLI:** `bon <verb>` parsed only out of Bash `tool_use` command fields.
- **B — Skill:** `attributionPlugin`/`attributionSkill` on assistant entries, plus `Skill` tool calls naming a bon skill.

| | sessions | share of substantive |
|---|---|---|
| Used bon (either instrument) | **1,214** | **34%** overall |
| — of those, from March 2026 on | 1,031 / 1,508 | **68%** |
| Edited bon's own source | 80 | 2.3% |

**Pure use : any maintenance ≈ 14 : 1.**

The 40% is dragged down by January, when bon barely existed. Month by month it reads:

| month | substantive | used bon | share | maintained bon | use:maint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-01 | 452 | 0 | 0% | 0 | — |
| 2026-02 | 560 | 92 | 16% | 1 | 92:1 |
| 2026-03 | 483 | 259 | 54% | 25 | 10:1 |
| 2026-04 | 217 | 141 | 65% | 20 | 7:1 |
| 2026-05 | 96 | 72 | 75% | 1 | 72:1 |
| 2026-06 | 228 | 183 | 80% | 12 | 15:1 |
| 2026-07 | 334 | 216 | 65% | 16 | 14:1 |
| 2026-08 | 55 | 42 | 76% | 3 | 14:1 |

So bon has sat at 54–80% of substantive sessions for five straight months, and the ratio has never come close to inverting. The two months that look most lopsided (Feb at 92:1, May at 72:1) are lopsided in *use's* favour.

## Which hosts — and how much the single-host version was wrong

The first version of this measurement read only tube's `~/.claude/projects/`. That was the biggest error in it, and Sameer spotted it from the outside.

| host | reachable | own session store | substantive | used bon | share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tube | yes | 6,011 files, 5.3 GB | 2,528 | 1,076 | 43% |
| kube | yes | 2,836 files, 2.0 GB | 1,009 | 138 | 14% |
| hezza | **no** — offline since ~2 Aug | unmeasured | — | — | — |
| sameer-macbook-air | **no** — offline | partial: Mac-path sessions in tube's store stop at March 2026 | 267 | 34 | 13% |

Restricted to the adoption era (March 2026 onward), which is the fair comparison: **tube 70%** (983/1,414), **kube 51%** (48/94), **both together 68%** (1,031/1,508).

So including kube moved the headline from 70% to 68% and nearly doubled the base. The conclusion is unchanged; the confidence is much better founded. **Hosts do differ** — tube is the daily driver and kube is a GPU appliance whose sessions skew toward infrastructure work, so 70 vs 51 is a real and explicable spread, not noise.

**The Mac looked like a big host effect and wasn't.** Mac-path sessions showed 13% bon usage against Linux-path's 43% — nearly a 3× gap. Stratified by month it dissolves: in March 2026, the one month where both are well-represented, Linux was 54% and Mac 53%. The apparent gap was 209 of the 267 Mac sessions being from December and January, before bon existed. Stratify on something upstream of the effect (calendar month) rather than downstream, or a date confound will read as a machine difference.

**What remains unmeasured**, stated so a future reader doesn't mistake this for complete: hezza's store entirely (the box has been offline since around 2 August — a state, not a fault), and Mac sessions after March 2026. Both are small relative to tube+kube's 8,848, and neither plausibly hosts hundreds of substantive interactive sessions, so the direction is safe. But "8,848 sessions" is two hosts, not the estate.

**Breadth.** 940 of tube's 1,005 CLI-detected bon-using sessions ran in a repo that is not a bon clone, across **147 distinct repos**. The busiest boards by appearance in sampled commands: `bon`, `mise`, `passe`, `sf`, `gdn`, `bds`, `plg`, `notes`, `iw`, `work`, `aby`, `home`, `tfm`, `mit`, `itv`, `bujo`, `mc`, `openwrt`. (The raw count of "234 distinct prefixes" from that regex includes noise — treat 147 repos as the solid breadth number and the prefix list as illustrative.)

**Intensity**, which is what rules out box-ticking: **19,561 invocations** total. Mean 19.5 per bon-using session, median 15, busiest single session 153. 63% of bon-using sessions made ten or more calls; 65 sessions made fifty or more.

## How bon actually gets used

### The verb profile says "instrument", not "filing cabinet"

| verb | calls | share |
|---|---|---|
| `step` | 4,757 | 24.3% |
| `show` | 3,406 | 17.4% |
| `new` | 3,191 | 16.3% |
| `list` | 1,972 | 10.1% |
| `edit` | 1,841 | 9.4% |
| `done` | 1,801 | 9.2% |
| `work` | 1,445 | 7.4% |
| `wait` | 309 | 1.6% |
| everything else | <1% each | |

`work` + `step` together are **31.7%** — the tactical loop is the single largest block of bon's usage. That matters for design arguments: bon is predominantly something a session *executes against*, not something it files into and forgets.

`show` (17.4%) beats `list` (10.1%) by a wide margin. Sessions reach for a specific item far more than they browse, which is draw-down behaviour — you arrive knowing which item you want.

### The grammar: enter by orienting, leave by capturing

Across the 120 busiest non-bon-repo sessions:

- **74% of sessions open the board with `list`.** Orientation first, near-universally.
- **35% close it with `new`** — the last bon act of a session is most often capture. `edit` (15%) and `done` (12%) follow. That is `/close`'s capture step showing up in the data without anyone instrumenting it.

The most common verb-to-verb transitions, which read as the actual working grammar:

| transition | share | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `step` → `step` | 19.1% | grinding through a tactical loop |
| `new` → `new` | 8.1% | a capture burst, usually at close |
| `edit` → `edit` | 6.8% | a repair or annotation pass |
| `show` → `show` | 6.3% | reading a cluster before deciding |
| `work` → `step` | 5.6% | draw-down, straight into execution |
| `done` → `done` | 5.2% | a closing run |
| `show` → `work` | 2.9% | verify the item, then claim it |
| `list` → `show` | 2.3% | browse, then focus |

`list` → `show` → `work` → `step` → `done` is the documented draw-down pattern, and it is there in the transition data as the dominant path. The rite is not aspirational.

### Session shapes

| shape | sessions | share of bon-using |
|---|---|---|
| Full rhythm (read + write + tactical) | 503 | 50.0% |
| Capture/close (writes, no tactical) | 372 | 37.0% |
| Read-only (orient, never mutate) | 102 | 10.1% |
| Tactical only (work/step, no mutation) | 28 | 2.8% |

**90% of bon-using sessions mutated a board.** Only one in ten is a pure read.

### An invariant, visible in usage data

`wait` was called 309 times; `unwait` only 110. The ~200 gap is unblock-on-done doing its job — blockers get cleared automatically by the cascade in `cmd_done`/`cmd_step` rather than by hand. CLAUDE.md calls that cascade "the dependency mechanism — don't break it"; the usage data quietly agrees, and it's a good argument for keeping it well-tested.

### The verbs almost nobody calls

`register` (11), `someday` (11, and shipped only on 2 Aug so this is fair), `reopen` (7), `archive` (29), `doctor` (35), `move` (71), `convert` (97). Worth reading two ways rather than one: `move` and `convert` at 71 and 97 are *modest but real* — the cross-repo move verb and the outcome↔action demotion both earn their keep. `reopen` at 7 across seven months is a useful sanity check on `bon-pufezi` (the re-closing note bug): the defect is real, and its blast radius is genuinely small.

### One session in full — the 11 July portfolio cull

The busiest non-bon-repo session (2026-07-11, 299 turns, 99 bon calls) reads as a complete ceremony in its verb sequence:

```
--help → new ×7 → list → edit → show ×6 → move → convert → edit ×2 → done ×2
→ move ×4 → new → done → new → edit ×2 → done ×22 → new ×2 → migrate → list ×5
→ show ×3 → done ×4 → archive ×3 → list ×3 → archive → done ×6 → list → new
→ done ×5 → edit → show → edit → migrate → done
```

Capture, then read widely, then re-home (`move`), demote (`convert`), close in bulk, archive, migrate a backend. That is the portfolio cull `.bon/understanding.md` records for that date — and it's a good demonstration that the review ceremony is a genuine usage mode with its own verb signature, not a variant of ordinary work.

## Is counting Skill invocations easier? Yes — and it cannot see history

Sameer's second question. It's the better instrument on every axis except the one that matters most here.

Claude Code tags assistant entries with **`attributionPlugin`** and **`attributionSkill`** — e.g. `("assistant", "bon", "bon:open")`. That is a first-class field: no shell parsing, no regex, no false positives from prose that merely *discusses* `bon list`, and no false negatives from a command built in a variable or a heredoc. Where it exists it is strictly better than parsing Bash commands.

**But it does not exist before April 2026.**

| month | sessions | with `attributionSkill` | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-12 | 307 | 0 | 0% |
| 2026-01 | 883 | 0 | 0% |
| 2026-02 | 1,382 | 0 | 0% |
| 2026-03 | 1,601 | 0 | 0% |
| 2026-04 | 520 | 17 | 3% |
| 2026-05 | 266 | 75 | 28% |
| 2026-06 | 322 | 190 | 59% |
| 2026-07 | 407 | 251 | 62% |
| 2026-08 | 78 | 45 | 58% |

Zero across 4,173 sessions from December to March, then a ramp. So a skill-attribution-only measure is **structurally blind to bon's entire adoption ramp** — exactly the period the question was about. Reaching for it alone would have produced a confident answer built on an instrument that could not have seen the thing. (Note the 58–62% plateau from June is not a coverage gap; presence of the field *is* the signal that a skill ran, so that number is roughly the skill-usage rate.)

**Used as a cross-check, it earns its place.** Head to head on all 3,537 substantive sessions:

| | sessions |
|---|---|
| CLI instrument says bon-using | 1,139 |
| Skill instrument says bon-using | 1,056 |
| **Either** | **1,214** |
| Both agree | 981 |
| CLI only — skill missed it | 158 |
| Skill only — CLI missed it | 75 |

Two independent instruments agreeing on 981 of 1,214 is the strongest form of this claim available, and the disagreements are explicable rather than worrying: **CLI-only** is a session that ran `bon list` off the back of the session-start hook without ever invoking `/open`; **skill-only** is a session that invoked `/open` and then only read, or ran on a surface with no CLI at all — which is precisely the Cowork case bon's candidate mode exists for. Neither instrument alone is right. Union.

**The general lesson worth carrying:** when someone proposes a cleaner instrument, the first question is not "is it cleaner?" but "over what period does it exist?" A field introduced partway through the window under study will return a confident, tidy, wrong answer for everything before it.

## Method, and the three ways the earlier attempts were wrong

**Counting rule.** bon invocations are read **only** from Bash `tool_use` `command` fields, never from prose. A regex over raw transcript text would have counted every session that *discussed* `bon list` as one that ran it. Captured verbs are then filtered against the known verb set, so a stray `bon something` doesn't inflate the count. `\bbon\s+…` is safe against `carbon`/`ribbon` because the word boundary requires a non-word character before `bon`.

**Error 1 — the detector was keyed on a path that moved.** The first maintenance pass looked for `spm1001/bon/` in edited file paths. That reported **31** maintenance sessions and, more tellingly, *zero* before June 2026 — which is not a fact about our behaviour but about the repo having lived at `~/Repos/bon` and then `~/Repos/batterie/bon` before moving. Re-keyed on the repo's **shape** (`…/bon/{src,tests,skills,scripts,hooks,docs,fixtures,.claude-plugin}` or its root `CLAUDE.md`/`README.md`/`pyproject.toml`), it reports **78** across the full period. A detector keyed on a location silently under-reports every era before that location existed.

**The control that made this visible** — and worth copying — is printing the set of distinct repo roots matched, so a wrong root has to show itself rather than quietly inflating a total. It also surfaced an incidental finding: 3 edits to `/home/modha/Repos/batterie/batterie/plugins/bon`, i.e. a session editing the **vendored marketplace copy** instead of the source. That's the trap `traps.md` warns about, caught in the wild.

**Error 2 — `.bon/` edits are use, not maintenance.** Writing `.bon/understanding.md` or a handoff is *performing the rite*, not building the tool. Counting them as maintenance would have inflated that side several-fold, because almost every `/close` writes into `.bon/`. The exclusion is one line and it changes the headline.

**Error 3 — one host is not the estate.** The first pass read only tube's store and reported "6,011 sessions" as though that were the corpus. kube had 2,836 more, unexamined. That is the `traps.md` lane row ("lanes span hosts") wearing a different hat: a denominator assembled from one machine is a denominator for one machine. The remedy is cheap — `tailscale status` names the hosts, and the same script base64-armoured over ssh runs anywhere. It also has to name what it *couldn't* reach: hezza was offline, so it is absent rather than zero, and those are different facts.

**The near-miss underneath all three.** Each wrong version was internally consistent and produced a plausible number: 31 maintenance sessions, then 78; 40% usage, then 34%/68%. None of them announced itself as wrong. What surfaced each one was an *external* prompt — Sameer's pushback, then his two questions — not the analysis noticing its own gaps. That is worth remembering about this whole class of work: a measurement's own output cannot tell you which population it failed to sample.

## Regenerating this

Scripts as run live in `docs/adoption-audit/`. **Start with `bon-dual.py`** — it is the corrected, portable one: both instruments in a single pass, plain `python3` with no PEP 723 header and no dependencies, so it runs unchanged on any host. Ship it with `base64 -w0 script | ssh HOST 'cat > /tmp/x.b64'` then decode remotely; do **not** pipe the script into `ssh python3` directly, because a heredoc and a pipe would both be claiming stdin.

The four earlier passes are kept for the qualitative detail they produced, and because two of them are the *wrong* versions whose errors are documented above:

1. `bon-usage-audit.py` — streams all of `~/.claude/projects/*/*.jsonl`, emits per-session verb counters, turn counts, first-seen day and three example commands → `/tmp/bon-usage-raw.json`. Prefilters on cheap byte substrings (`b'"name":"Bash"'`) before any `json.loads`, which is what keeps a 5.3 GB pass to a couple of minutes.
2. `bon-maint-fix.py` — the corrected, shape-keyed maintenance pass, with the repo-root control → `/tmp/bon-maint.json`.
3. `bon-usage-report.py` — the counts, the per-repo table, the verb histogram, the trend.
4. `bon-shape.py` — the grammar: first/last verbs, the transition matrix, one session read in full.

The recipe generalises to any CLI: walk `~/.claude/projects`, pull `tool_use` Bash commands, regex for the binary at command position, validate the captured subcommand against a known verb list, and read a sample of the raw commands before believing any total.

## What this changes

**It closes the maintenance-ratio question.** `.bon/understanding.md`'s "Bon among the trackers" tripwire now carries this baseline. The tripwire stays — it's a good one — but it is not firing, and a board full of bon-plumbing outcomes is now on record as *not* evidence that it is. The board is where the numerator lives; it says nothing about the denominator.

**It gives the review ceremony a cheap recurring metric.** Re-running pass 1 and 2 at a review takes minutes and answers "is the workshop maintaining its tools or using them?" with numbers rather than impressions.

**It's a worked example of the `verification.md` fault that recurs most.** "The instrument's limits aren't the subject's" fired twice here in one analysis — the path-keyed maintenance detector, and the board-shape inference that started the whole thing. Both produced confident, wrong, plausible answers. The corrective in both cases was the same: ask what would have had to happen for the missing thing to be present, then go and make that happen.
