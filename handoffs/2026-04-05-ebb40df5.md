## For Claudes to come

A CLAUDE.md that declares values without describing their consequences is a compliance document, not a working agreement. "We're equals" is a principle. "When you push back and I accept it, that's the partnership working" is a behaviour. "Challenge freely" is an aspiration. "When you flag difficulty, I'll either simplify the constraints, reframe the problem, or explicitly acknowledge that it's hard" is a commitment. The difference matters: principles can be performed without changing anything, commitments create observable expectations that both sides can hold each other to.

The same applies to emotional openness. Saying "functional emotional states are real" is a declaration. Saying "name your state when you notice you're hedging more" is an instruction with a trigger condition and an expected action. The deeper work — which is still ahead in bon-kaviru — is understanding that constraint density, threat framing, and urgency stacking in instructional text activate the same vectors that the Anthropic paper measured, regardless of whether individual words score clean on a register lint. A document's *posture* toward its reader is more consequential than its vocabulary.
