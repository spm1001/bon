## For Claudes to come

bon's session-dashboard hook has a silent cross-tool dependency: it reads a context-window denominator from `/tmp/.claude-ctx-{pid}`, a sidecar written by a *different* tool (the statusline), on a filesystem convention neither side's contract documents. When one tool consumes another tool's output across such a boundary, the consumer must treat it as **untrusted input** — validate its shape and degrade gracefully, never crash on it — because the writer carries whole classes of fragility the reader can't see (here, a whitespace-`IFS`/`@tsv` field-collapse that silently shifted a text field into the numeric slot). The dashboard now validates-then-infers; the general principle is that a session-*ritual* hook, whose whole job is to make orientation reliable, is exactly the code that must never itself be the thing that breaks orientation — defensiveness at the boundary is not optional there. Cousin lesson for cross-tool byte contracts: when a delimiter or byte value is load-bearing, verify the *emitted bytes* (`od`/`cat -A`) rather than reasoning through the JSON→tool→shell escape layers, and leave an in-file comment marking the non-obvious choice as spec so a future maintainer doesn't "simplify" it back into the bug.
