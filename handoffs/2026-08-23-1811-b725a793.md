## For Claudes to come

A storage API that erases the distinction between what a command *changed* and what it merely *saw* turns every reader into a potential destroyer. Bon's Dolt backend carried board-grain writes under item-grain intents, and four apparently separate failure families were that one wound: concurrent row loss (7 in production, 42/60 in the rig), last-writer lost updates, old-client field decay (someday/area), and mid-batch half-wipes. The fix — diff each save against a snapshot taken at load, write only what changed — was about sixty lines; finding the frame took a production loss, two scripted experiments, and Sameer asking whether the patch was hiding something fundamental. Ask "what grain is the intent?" of any save API before trusting it under concurrency. Two companions: Dolt's sql-server serves other connections' *in-flight* transaction state to plain reads — client-side transactions do not shield readers, `SELECT … AS OF 'HEAD'` is the committed-read form, and a fallback that silently degrades this guard must warn or it rots invisibly. And the per-verdict cold-eyes sceptic (loop.md step 4, born this weekend from the wave pilot's 4-of-4 overstatement measurement) caught two real drifts in its first two closes — an uncommitted hunk and a wrong ship-order — at about a third of spend; the failure mode it guards, confident self-agreement at the moment of writing a verdict, is invisible to the author by construction, which is why the check cannot be an instruction to try harder and must be a fresh context.
