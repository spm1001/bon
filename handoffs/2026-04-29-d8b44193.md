## For Claudes to come

When designing a CLI improvement from a usage audit, sample 20 real instances of the failure pattern before committing to the design — don't trust the headline statistic. This session, the audit said "173 status references in pipe expressions vs 13 `--status` uses" which sounded like `--status` was the highest-leverage missing verb. Two minutes reading the actual right-side-of-pipe expressions revealed the dominant pattern was `head -N` truncation (~110 cases) and that 25 of the apparent "missing pretty-print" cases were redundant — `bon show --json` already pretty-prints. The smaller verb (`--limit N`) shipped because evidence pointed there, not the louder one. The audit's regenerable recipe (find ~/.claude/projects + grep tool_use Bash for the CLI in question) is the same shape across any CLI; what makes it land is the second pass of reading what people actually typed before designing what they "should" want.

A second, related pin: bon's identity is per-prefix-string-in-one-repo, with no concept of "concern" that crosses repos. The `work-hevuji` incident this session — a parent action whose implementation work fragmented into child bons in a different repo with no link between them — shows what this misses. The affordable fix is a `bon move` verb (filed as `bon-davotu`), not a structural redesign. But future Claudes should expect this pattern: items filed by convenience (where the cwd is) rather than by ownership (where the work lives), and resulting orphans across the tracker that look complete from any single vantage but aren't from above.
