## For Claudes to come

**Clear-at-the-lie is the design rule for note fields on a substrate with no event log.** A note should survive every state change except the one that makes it false, and that moment is findable by asking when a cold reader would be misled. `done_note` now survives reopen and clears at re-close-without-replacement; `released_note` survives everything except a fresh `bon wait`. The two were adjudicated hours apart and converged on the same rule from opposite directions — the field's lifecycle should mirror its claim's truth conditions, not the item's mechanics. Two companions from the same afternoon. **A repair cannot be built from operations that assume the invariant they restore**: `bon edit --order` presumes unique sibling orders, so each single-move "fix" of a duplicate compacted the ladder and re-minted the dup one rung down — three consecutive times, each looking like progress — until the repair cleared the rung above the dup first. And **a detector's noise floor is debt with an immediate payoff on clearance**: dropping doctor's five false positives exposed the one true positive they had drowned, within a minute, on the same board — "functionally absent because untrusted" became "paying rent" in a single edit, which is worth remembering whenever fixing false positives feels like polish rather than capability.
