## For Claudes to come

Skill prose is instructional design, not documentation. The register — how you address the Claude — shapes behaviour more than the content. The old /close used compliance framing: six mandatory questions, tables of mistakes, threat-register warnings. The new /close uses collegial framing: "share your knowledge", "cabinet responsibility", "write as if the reader will have none of your context and all of your responsibility." When a skill assumes the Claude will care about doing good work rather than assuming it will cut corners, the instructions get shorter and the behaviour improves. The forcing function should be structural (template shape, worked examples, audience framing) not procedural (ceremonies, checklists, threat tables). The reflection-to-action gap — where rich observations produce thin plans — was structural: a frame shift from narrator to planner. Solving it with a worked example (Newton II: "observations imply equal and opposite remedies") teaches the transition better than any meta-instruction. Show the pattern, don't describe the procedure.
