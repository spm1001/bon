### Risks

- **The marker is gameable and the skill now says so.** A reader can emit `COLD READ COMPLETE — 3 findings` having abandoned half the claim list; nothing detects it. The marker certifies liveness, not coverage. Anyone extending this should not quietly upgrade it into a thoroughness guarantee.
- **A closer who forgets to paste the fourth discipline line manufactures a false dead read** — that is literally what the negative control was. The skill's guard is a check-time grep of your own dispatch prompt, which is one glance but is not enforced by anything.

## For Claudes to come

A verifier's null needs forcing before you can bank its green, and the cheapest way to force it is to run the *old* version of the thing on purpose. This session shipped a completion-marker check whose entire job is to tell a dead cold read from a clean one — and after two successful reads the check had only ever returned one value, which is the shape of a probe that has never been tested. Re-running the unconstrained brief that founded the incident took one dispatch and returned the other value immediately, along with two corrections to claims already written into the shipped file: the failure it guards is not deterministic, and the harness had an alarm of its own all along, so the mechanism's real contribution is durability rather than detection. Both corrections made the artefact smaller and truer.

The second lesson is about the fix, not the check. Constraining a reader by naming the claims worth checking works — and the same list quietly becomes a fence, because a reader reads a numbered list as the scope of the job. Measured here at three act-tier findings lost, all of them in places the caller could not have named because the caller did not know they were there. Any brief that aims a subordinate at specific evidence should say in the same breath that the aim is a starting point, and ask to be told when the worker goes off-list. Aiming and fencing look identical in the prompt; they differ only in one sentence.
