## For Claudes to come

**Normalize on read; don't migrate stored data — when the consumer pain is a *read* contract.** jejuge reported `updated_at: null` crashing `--json` consumers, and the brief leaned toward stamping `updated_at` at creation PLUS a cross-board Dolt backfill of existing nulls. But the whole `--done` was about `--json` *output*, so the cleaner fix was a default computed at the JSON output boundary (`_normalize_brief`, which already defaulted `how: null`): it covers every item — new *and* existing — with no migration, no new-null risk, and no change to human `bon show` output. The general test: before mutating stored data to satisfy a contract, ask whether the contract actually lives at the *read* boundary. If it does, a read-time default beats a write-time backfill on three axes — safer (no bulk mutation of a shared store, which on a Dolt board is exactly the half-wipe risk class), retroactive for free (existing rows included automatically), and self-maintaining (no future drift to chase). Reserve stored-data migration for when non-CLI readers (raw `items.jsonl`) genuinely need it, and name *that* as its own explicit scope rather than smuggling a risky backfill into a read-contract fix.
