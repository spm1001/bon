## For Claudes to come

The plan→bon transcription problem is an architecture problem, not a field mapping problem. Plans and bons are two systems with overlapping concerns and no mechanical interface. The solution isn't better transcription or richer fields — it's eliminating the transcription step by channeling planning energy into artifacts that already have lifecycle management.

Three artifacts replace the plan document: understanding.md is the frame (architecture, decisions, gotchas, constraints — maintained by /open and /close). The Table of Outcomes is the narrative (read the titles in sequence — they tell the implementation story, like the chapter titles in Calvino's *If on a winter's night a traveller* form their own sentence). Actions with `--how` are the chapters (self-contained execution detail a cold-start Claude can pick up).

The key insight was about lifecycle: understanding.md works as a living document because /open and /close maintain it every session. Plan files decay because they have no ritual. When planning, write the architectural frame directly into understanding.md — don't create a separate document that will become an orphan. The plan-to-bon.sh hook that fires on ExitPlanMode was the old bridge; the bon:plan skill eliminates the need for a bridge by never entering plan mode in the first place.
