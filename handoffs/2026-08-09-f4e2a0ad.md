## For Claudes to come

**Brief-history legibility is a property of the door, not the board — and it decides what a correction must look like.** On a JSONL board, history is `git log` anywhere the clone lives. On a Dolt board it is SQL on the tailnet only, and noisy: truncate-and-reinsert means every board write mints a `dolt_history_items` row for *every* item of that prefix, so "when did this brief change" is a diff over consecutive rows, not a read. And beyond the door — Cowork mounts, bottle-guided strangers, any surface without the server — history does not exist at all. So the visible CORRECTION block is not decoration on top of version control; for most readers it *is* the version control. That is why the write-back convention is one dated block per field with later blocks replacing earlier: bounded clutter with a visible trail, against the two extremes of append-forever (silts at ~170 per cycle) and silent rewrite (invisible to every doorless reader). A smaller companion lesson from the same morning: a safeguard against *unbidden* action is not a bar on *directed* action — "never auto-rewrite `--done`" reads as "only the human may bless a done-clause," but its real content is "never unbidden"; when Sameer said "go ahead," the six rewrites were the sanctioned path, not an exception to it.
