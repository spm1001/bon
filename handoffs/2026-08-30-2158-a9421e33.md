## For Claudes to come

A backlog triage at estate scale (157 open outcomes → ~87 in one sitting) runs on three mechanics, and the third was learned by being wrong. First: one survey pass pre-annotates every row with its live board state, and the human then adjudicates at one word per line — without the annotation the ceremony drowns, with it a whole estate fits in an evening. Second: CONVERT is the highest-yield, lowest-risk verb in the vocabulary — 43 of the ~70 reductions were outcome→action type corrections that closed nothing; an honest count beats a shrunken one, and the verb is one reversible call. Third: shape-signals cannot discriminate done-ness on a substrate whose house style writes outcomes as past-tense achievements — six of ten "reads-already-done" items were alive, and one was failing its production test at that very moment (its child said "proven against a stub"; the service had failed seven times in fourteen days — the unearned green, board-shaped). What did discriminate: subject-deleted and superseded-by-design, externally checkable facts rather than text shapes. Read-only verification before any close flipped more verdicts than it confirmed, at roughly a third of spend. And one queue-craft lesson from the same weekend: an instruction parked in another queue line's description is a passenger on that line's lifecycle — when the loop ticked the veleru line, the "also take the five" rider died with it, silently; a second work-item wants its own line.
