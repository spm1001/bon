## For Claudes to come

A duplicate bon prefix has two origins, and they want different repairs. The first is a squat: two unrelated boards independently choose the same word, no id actually collides, and the fix is cosmetic urgency ahead of a real one. The second is a transfer, and it is the nastier of the two — when a repo carrying a `.bon/` is moved, forked or re-homed and both copies survive, the board travels with the code, so the two boards begin life holding byte-identical item lines. The ids do not merely risk colliding; they collide from the first minute, silently, and they keep colliding for every item minted before the split. Nothing errors, because both files are internally valid and neither knows the other exists. The discriminating question is not "do these boards share a prefix" but "do these boards share ids", and it is one set intersection: if the overlap is non-empty you are looking at a transfer, and the overlap is the part that was already broken.

That distinction changes the repair on both ends. A transfer collision is *cheaper* to clean than it looks, because the surviving board holds verbatim copies of every shared item — so the historical citations of those shared ids still resolve correctly after the rename, and need no edit anywhere. Only ids minted after the split are genuinely orphaned, and there are usually one or two. Check the intersection before touching a single external file; the instinct to rewrite every citation is expensive and mostly wrong.

The second thing worth carrying is what a duplicate prefix does to the instrument that reports it. The estate survey keys items by id, so two boards sharing an open id have that item counted once — the survey silently *undercounts*, and it under-reports one of the two boards while the duplicate-prefix warning sits right there in its own stderr saying only that citations are ambiguous. So the warning understates itself: alongside it, the open counts beside it are wrong, and the board that loses the coin-toss looks emptier than it is. Clearing one such collision moved the estate-wide open count by one, and that plus-one was not a new item — it was an open item that had been invisible for weeks becoming visible again. When a survey warns about identity, distrust its arithmetic in the same breath, and expect the counts to move when you fix it. A number that changes when you repair a naming fault was never a count of the world; it was a count of what the instrument could tell apart.
