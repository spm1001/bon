And a window that starts more than 60 s in the future exits 2 with `MOTION_ERROR=window start … is in the future … a local time passed as UTC?` instead of printing zeroes — that is the tell of the incident's exact shape, and at the incident's own close (20:2xZ) a 21:17Z start would have tripped it. `_utc_to_epoch` carries the BSD `date -u -j -f` form beside GNU's, as `_local_to_utc` does; if neither parses, the future check is skipped and the MOTION_SINCE line says so.

## For Claudes to come

**A string compare across two timestamp shapes is not a time compare, even when both are ISO-ish.** `"2026-09-13 21:17"` sorts before `"2026-09-13T00:00:00"` because space precedes T; the same instant in the other shape sorts after everything that day. When a script prints a value for a human to copy back, parse it back by shape and disclose the conversion, or you get two silent wrong answers, one in each direction.

**A repro line in a brief is a hypothesis about what the reporter typed.** Run the shapes before writing the test; the shape that fails red may not be the one the brief names.
