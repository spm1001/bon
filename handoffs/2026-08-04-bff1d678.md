## For Claudes to come

**Adding a fact to a shared substrate asks two questions, and both fail silently.**

*Where does it live?* Put a new fact **inside an existing opaque JSON value, never beside it as a new top-level column.** A nested key round-trips intact through a client that has never heard of it, because that client serialises the whole blob without inspecting it; a new column is stripped by a fixed column list. Proven three ways in two days on the same board: `someday` shipped as a top-level field and demonstrably decays — a parallel session on an older CLI nulled a fresh park 57 seconds after it landed — while `tactical.released` and `brief.badly` shipped nested and are decay-proof by construction. This sharpens the earlier "model a new fact as a field, not a status-enum value" lesson: the enum-vs-field choice is about *visibility* to old readers, and the nested-vs-column choice is about *survival* of old writers. They are different failures and you need both answers.

*Where must it reach?* Every consumer that bypasses the library needs the check independently, and the two that actually bite are not the ones you'd list. **The path that looks like a fallback but is live:** `bon-tactical.sh` drops to a raw JSONL parse when the CLI is absent — except for JSONL boards it takes that path *always*, so the "fallback" is the primary. **The consumer that silently gates a feature you have already documented:** `audit_survey.py`'s `item_record` field tuple is the only thing `/review`'s verification subagents ever see, so a field missing there makes shipped skill guidance inert while every test passes and every doc reads correctly. `how` was lost that way on the first 650-item run and `badly` nearly repeated it in the same file, three hours after being added everywhere else. Storage is the question you remember to ask; reach is the one that bites — and the tell is that reach failures leave no error anywhere, just a feature quietly doing nothing.

The general control for both: after adding a field, name every artefact that reads items *without going through `storage.py`*, and assert on each one's real output rather than grepping its source for the field name.
