## For Claudes to come

A dispatch queue for a human-and-agents partnership wants exactly two bits of state per line, and neither is a field: WHOSE MOVE IS IT (lane membership — Sameer's "Newton's Laws of Todoist") and WHAT'S NEXT (position, carried by his drag-order, which costs him nothing and is unambiguous to a machine). The 2026-08-23 ceremony proved the operating consequences. Triage and sorting fuse — the bucket IS the verdict (tick / yours / up next / backlog / dead), so a 43-line backlog resolves in five conversational clusters at one word per line — but only if every line arrives pre-annotated with its cited bon's live state from a single survey pass; without the annotation the human triages from memory and the ceremony drowns. Two corollaries with teeth. First, a steer that doesn't fit the line goes in the task's description field, and any reader that jq's only `content` is blind to it — check what your reader actually reads before parking load-bearing context in a side field (the budget line's "needs a Fable" was invisible to loop.md's dispatcher until the read-the-description sentence landed). Second, a live ceremony has two writers on one queue — the human dragging in the app, the session writing through the API — and the races are silent (a line lands in the wrong lane and looks deliberate); batch your writes, announce your write-windows, and stop reordering the moment the human's drags start.
