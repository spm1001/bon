## For Claudes to come

**Bon's brief schema is the GTD Natural Planning Model wearing flags — and knowing that resolves design questions the flags alone can't.** Verified mapping (2026-08-01, sources on bon-zijazi's --how): `--why` = phase 1 purpose, `--done` = phase 2 outcome visioning, `--how` = phase 4 organising, `--what` = phase 5 next actions, and phase 3 brainstorming is deliberately unstored (the conversation is its home). The load-bearing discovery: bon collapsed phase 1 to its purpose half and DROPPED PRINCIPLES — Allen's "I would give others totally free rein to do this as long as they…", written by the delegator before handover so that handover is safe at all. That absence is exactly the falsifier gap (adopted 2026-08-01 as the `badly` field, bon-meliga): `--done` asks "how do we know it's complete", which an Opus-5-generation model satisfies literally and by construction; principles ask "how would we know it went wrong", and only work when the implementer didn't author them. The general lesson for schema questions on this board: when a brief field feels missing or overloaded, check the NPM mapping FIRST — the model has survived forty years of delegation practice, and where bon deviates from it (dropped principles; the `--what` gravity well absorbing `--how`'s organising role), the deviation is usually the bug. Someday/Maybe is the same shape one level up: GTD treats it as a reviewed list with revisit conditions, bon had only a title prefix, and the cost was measured — an outcome whose gate had cleared sat unnoticed for seven weeks (bon-majoca).

**Decision items work when the brief argues against itself.** The nibuto pattern — each decision's `--how` carrying both sides, counter-evidence, and the case for no-change — produced three adjudications in one exchange, where the same questions as bare proposals would have read as homework. The mechanism: Sameer finds his view by reacting to something concrete, and a brief that already contains the strongest objection gives him something to push against without having to construct the opposition himself. Corollary observed twice in one conversation: his naive-sounding questions ("what is candidate mode?", "so we'd have four fields?") were verdicts in disguise — unrecognition of a shipped feature IS demand evidence, and a field-count worry IS a schema smell. Treat them as probes to run, not confusions to correct.
